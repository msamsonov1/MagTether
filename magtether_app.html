Electrodynamic Bare-Wire Tether Physics & Dynamic State Observer

Rigorous mathematical framework governing Orbital-Motion-Limited (OML) plasma electron collection, geomagnetic Lorentz deceleration, and pendular libration stabilization.

1. Induced Electromotive Force & Lorentz Drag Force

As a conductive tether of length $\mathbf{L}$ traverses the terrestrial magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ at hypervelocity orbital speed $\mathbf{v}$, it cuts geomagnetic field lines, inducing an electromotive force (EMF) along its conductive length:

$$\mathcal{E}_{\text{induced}} = \int_{0}^{L} \left( \mathbf{v}_{\text{orb}} \times \mathbf{B}_{\text{geo}} \right) \cdot d\mathbf{l} = v B L \sin(\alpha)$$

When the circuit is closed via ambient ionospheric plasma collection at the anodic upper segment and active electron emission at the cathodic lower end, a current $I(s)$ circulates, generating a Lorentz braking force $\mathbf{F}_L$:

$$\mathbf{F}_L = \int_{0}^{L} I(s) \left( d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B}_{\text{geo}} \right) \quad \implies \quad F_{\text{drag}} \approx I_{\text{avg}} L B_{\perp}$$

This continuous electromagnetic drag removes orbital mechanical energy without expendable chemical propellants, causing continuous altitude decay ($dh/dt$).

2. Orbital-Motion-Limited (OML) Bare-Wire Current Collection

Unlike insulated tethers requiring massive end-body contactors, a bare conductive cylinder collects electrons directly along its positively biased upper section according to OML cylindrical probe physics:

$$i_{\text{OML}}(s) = \frac{d I}{d s} = \frac{2 r_{\text{wire}} q_e N_e}{\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2 q_e \Delta V(s)}{m_e}}$$

Integrating potential $\Delta V(s)$ from the cathodic emitter ($s=0$) to the anodic bias reversal point ($s=s_0$) reveals that current increases with the $3/2$ power of tether length:

$$I(s) = \frac{4}{3} e N_e r_{\text{wire}} \sqrt{\frac{2 e}{m_e}} \left( E_{\text{ind}} \right)^{1/2} s^{3/2}$$

Day/night solar terminator crossings induce sudden 10x drops in ambient ionospheric electron density $N_e$, causing dangerous drops in induced tension if unmonitored.

3. Non-Linear Libration Mechanics & State Observer

The tether experiences gravity-gradient torque aligning it along the local vertical, perturbed by the non-conservative Lorentz torque. The in-plane libration angle $\theta$ follows the non-linear equation of motion:

$$\ddot{\theta} + 3 \omega_0^2 \sin\theta \cos\theta = \frac{3 \mathbf{F}_L}{2 m_{\text{tether}} L} \cos\theta - 2 \omega_0 \frac{\dot{L}}{L} \sin\theta$$

The flight software implements an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) observer monitoring $(B, v)$ states. When observed tension feedback deviates from the theoretical Lorentz baseline $\Delta F_L = F_{\text{observed}} - I L B$:

$$I_{\text{emitter}}(t) = I_{\text{nominal}} - K_p \dot{\theta} - K_d \left( \ddot{\theta} - \ddot{\theta}_{\text{des}} \right) \cdot \operatorname{sgn}(B_{\perp})$$

By modulating active hollow-cathode electron discharge at the base, the algorithm applies stabilizing counter-torques, eliminating chaotic resonance before tension drops below zero (slack snapping).
